# 1. Technology & Information Technology

## 1.1 Software Development

**What the work involves.** Software developers design, build and maintain the programs that run businesses, devices and services. The work turns requirements into working, tested, maintainable code, from small utilities to large distributed platforms.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Translate product or business requirements into technical designs
- Write, review and refactor code in one or more programming languages
- Write automated tests and fix defects
- Use version control, code review and continuous integration
- Document systems and estimate work in agile planning sessions

**Working environment.** Mostly office-based or remote, at a computer, inside product teams of engineers, designers and product managers. Work runs in sprints. Deadlines around releases can be intense, and some roles include on-call support.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* enjoy logical problem-solving, can concentrate for long periods, are patient when debugging, and like learning new tools continually.
- *Think twice if you:* dislike prolonged screen time, find ambiguity or constant change stressful, or want work with visible physical results.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in computer science, software engineering or a related field is common. Bootcamp or self-taught routes are accepted when you have a strong portfolio.
- *Key skills:* Programming (e.g., Python, Java, C#, JavaScript), data structures and algorithms, Git, testing, problem decomposition, teamwork.
- *Credentials & experience:* A portfolio of projects or open-source contributions matters most. Cloud or language certifications are optional extras.

## 1.2 Web Development

**What the work involves.** Web developers build websites and web applications that run in browsers. They combine page structure, visual styling, interactivity and server connections to deliver fast, accessible online experiences.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Build responsive pages and web applications from designs
- Implement interactive features with JavaScript frameworks
- Connect sites to APIs, content management systems or databases
- Optimise performance, accessibility and search visibility
- Test across browsers and devices, and maintain live sites

**Working environment.** Agencies, in-house digital teams, start-ups or freelance practice, often remote. Projects can be short and client-driven, with frequent feedback cycles and launch deadlines.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* like seeing results quickly, care about both logic and visual detail, and enjoy working with designers and clients.
- *Think twice if you:* dislike frequent client changes or fast-moving frameworks, or prefer deep, long-horizon engineering problems.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree optional. Bootcamps, online courses and self-study are common routes.
- *Key skills:* HTML, CSS, JavaScript/TypeScript, a framework (React, Vue, Angular), basic back-end and APIs, Git, accessibility and SEO basics.
- *Credentials & experience:* A live portfolio of sites is essential. Freelance or agency experience helps.

## 1.3 Mobile Application Development

**What the work involves.** Mobile developers create apps for smartphones and tablets, either natively for iOS and Android or with cross-platform frameworks. They focus on smooth, intuitive, battery- and data-efficient experiences.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Build app screens, navigation and device integrations (camera, GPS, notifications)
- Connect apps to back-end services and handle offline data
- Optimise performance, memory use and app size
- Test on multiple devices and OS versions
- Prepare releases and follow App Store and Google Play guidelines

**Working environment.** Product companies, agencies and start-ups, office or remote. Release cycles are tied to store reviews, and user ratings give fast, public feedback on your work.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* care about polished user experience, enjoy working with hardware features, and like shipping products people use daily.
- *Think twice if you:* are frustrated by platform rules, frequent OS updates or device fragmentation.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Computer science or a related degree is helpful but not mandatory.
- *Key skills:* Swift/SwiftUI or Kotlin/Jetpack Compose, or Flutter/React Native; REST APIs; local storage; mobile UI/UX principles; debugging tools.
- *Credentials & experience:* Published apps in the stores are the strongest evidence of ability.

## 1.4 Front-End Development

**What the work involves.** Front-end developers build everything users see and interact with in a web or app interface. They turn designs into accessible, responsive, high-performance user interfaces.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Turn design mock-ups into reusable UI components
- Manage application state and data fetching
- Make interfaces accessible (WCAG) and responsive
- Improve load speed and rendering performance
- Maintain design systems and write UI tests

**Working environment.** Product teams working closely with UX/UI designers and back-end engineers. Usually office or remote, with an agile rhythm and frequent design reviews.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* have an eye for visual detail, empathise with users, and enjoy the meeting point of design and code.
- *Think twice if you:* dislike pixel-level precision or browser quirks, or prefer purely algorithmic work.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree optional. Strong practical skills are what count.
- *Key skills:* HTML, CSS, JavaScript/TypeScript, React or a similar framework, accessibility, browser dev tools, testing libraries, design tools like Figma.
- *Credentials & experience:* A portfolio of interfaces and contributions to component libraries.

## 1.5 Back-End Development

**What the work involves.** Back-end developers build the server-side logic, databases and APIs behind applications. They make sure data is processed correctly, securely and at scale.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Design and build APIs and business logic
- Model data and write efficient database queries
- Integrate third-party services and message queues
- Secure systems through authentication, authorisation and input validation
- Monitor, scale and troubleshoot services in production

**Working environment.** Engineering teams in tech firms, banks, e-commerce and the public sector. Work is screen-based, and some roles include on-call rotations for production incidents.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* enjoy systems thinking, data and reliability, and like solving invisible but critical problems.
- *Think twice if you:* want visual, user-facing output or dislike being responsible for systems outside working hours.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Computer science or a related degree is common.
- *Key skills:* A server language (Java, Go, Python, C#, Node.js), SQL and NoSQL databases, API design, caching, security fundamentals, cloud basics.
- *Credentials & experience:* Cloud developer certifications (AWS, Azure, GCP) are useful. Production experience is highly valued.

## 1.6 Full-Stack Development

**What the work involves.** Full-stack developers work across both front end and back end, delivering complete features end to end. They are especially valued in start-ups and small teams.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Build user interfaces and the APIs that serve them
- Design database schemas and data flows
- Deploy and maintain applications in the cloud
- Fix issues anywhere in the stack
- Make pragmatic architecture decisions for speed and quality

**Working environment.** Start-ups, agencies, small product teams and freelance work. Expect broad responsibility, context switching and fast iteration.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* are curious about how everything fits together, like variety, and enjoy owning features from idea to launch.
- *Think twice if you:* prefer deep specialisation or find constant context switching draining.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree, bootcamp or self-taught routes.
- *Key skills:* JavaScript/TypeScript, a front-end framework, a back-end framework, SQL, Git, cloud deployment, basic DevOps.
- *Credentials & experience:* End-to-end portfolio projects. Start-up experience is attractive to employers.

## 1.7 Software Testing & Quality Assurance

**What the work involves.** QA professionals make sure software works as intended before and after release. They combine manual exploratory testing with automated test suites to catch defects early.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Analyse requirements and write test plans and test cases
- Do manual, exploratory and regression testing
- Build and maintain automated test frameworks
- Log, track and verify defects with developers
- Run performance, security or accessibility checks

**Working environment.** Embedded in development teams in office or remote settings. Workload often peaks before releases.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* are meticulous, sceptical in a constructive way, and enjoy finding what others miss.
- *Think twice if you:* find repetitive checking tedious or dislike delivering bad news to colleagues.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* IT-related degree or diploma is helpful. Career changers are common.
- *Key skills:* Test design, bug tracking (Jira), automation (Selenium, Cypress, Playwright), API testing (Postman), basic programming, SQL.
- *Credentials & experience:* ISTQB Foundation or Advanced certification is widely recognised.

## 1.8 IT Support & Technical Services

**What the work involves.** IT support specialists keep people's computers, accounts, software and devices working. They are the first line of help when technology fails.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Respond to help-desk tickets, calls and walk-ins
- Install, configure and repair hardware and software
- Manage user accounts, passwords and access
- Troubleshoot networks, printers and email
- Document solutions and escalate complex issues

**Working environment.** Offices, schools, hospitals, managed-service providers or remote help desks. Shift work is possible, and the role is people-facing and often interrupted.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* are patient, service-minded, calm under pressure, and enjoy hands-on fixing.
- *Think twice if you:* dislike dealing with frustrated users or repeated interruptions.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Diploma or vocational IT qualification. A degree is not always required.
- *Key skills:* Windows/macOS, Active Directory/Entra ID, networking basics, Microsoft 365, troubleshooting, clear communication.
- *Credentials & experience:* CompTIA A+, Network+, ITIL Foundation, Microsoft certifications.

## 1.9 Systems Administration

**What the work involves.** System administrators install, configure and maintain the servers, operating systems and core services an organisation depends on. They keep those systems secure, patched and available.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Manage Linux/Windows servers and virtualisation platforms
- Apply patches, updates and security hardening
- Run backups, recovery and disaster recovery plans
- Monitor performance and resolve outages
- Automate routine tasks with scripts

**Working environment.** Data centres, server rooms and remote management consoles. Maintenance windows often fall at nights or weekends, and on-call duty is common.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* are methodical, reliable and calm in emergencies, and enjoy keeping things running smoothly.
- *Think twice if you:* want predictable hours or dislike being accountable for outages.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* IT degree or diploma, or equivalent experience.
- *Key skills:* Linux and Windows Server, scripting (Bash, PowerShell), networking, virtualisation (VMware, Hyper-V), backups, security basics.
- *Credentials & experience:* RHCSA/RHCE, Microsoft Azure Administrator, CompTIA Linux+/Server+.

## 1.10 Cloud Computing & Infrastructure

**What the work involves.** Cloud engineers design, build and run infrastructure on platforms like AWS, Azure and Google Cloud. They balance scalability, reliability, security and cost.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Design cloud architectures and network topologies
- Provision resources using infrastructure-as-code
- Migrate workloads from on-premises to cloud
- Monitor, optimise and control cloud costs
- Apply identity, security and compliance controls

**Working environment.** Tech companies, consultancies, banks and governments. Highly remote-friendly and project-based, with strong demand.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* think in systems, enjoy automation, and keep up with fast-evolving services.
- *Think twice if you:* dislike continual retraining or abstract, configuration-heavy work.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* IT or computer science degree, or equivalent experience.
- *Key skills:* AWS/Azure/GCP, Terraform, networking, Linux, containers (Docker, Kubernetes), security, scripting.
- *Credentials & experience:* AWS Solutions Architect, Azure Administrator/Architect, Google Professional Cloud Architect.

## 1.11 DevOps & Site Reliability Engineering

**What the work involves.** DevOps and SRE engineers connect development and operations. They automate how software is built, deployed and monitored so services ship quickly and stay reliable.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Build and maintain CI/CD pipelines
- Containerise and orchestrate applications
- Define service-level objectives and monitoring/alerting
- Lead incident response and blameless post-mortems
- Automate infrastructure and reduce manual toil

**Working environment.** Engineering organisations of all sizes, often remote. On-call rotations and high-pressure incident handling are part of the role.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* love automation and reliability, stay calm during incidents, and enjoy collaborating across teams.
- *Think twice if you:* dislike out-of-hours pager duty or high-stakes troubleshooting.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Computer science or IT degree, or a developer/sysadmin background.
- *Key skills:* Linux, Git, CI/CD tools (GitHub Actions, GitLab, Jenkins), Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform, observability (Prometheus, Grafana), scripting.
- *Credentials & experience:* CKA/CKAD, AWS DevOps Engineer, HashiCorp Terraform Associate.

## 1.12 Enterprise IT & Systems Integration

**What the work involves.** Enterprise IT specialists implement, connect and maintain large business systems such as ERP, CRM and HR platforms. They make sure data flows correctly between departments and applications.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Gather business requirements and configure enterprise software (SAP, Oracle, Salesforce, Dynamics)
- Build integrations through APIs, middleware or ETL
- Manage system upgrades, migrations and testing
- Train users and support the change process
- Manage vendors and licences

**Working environment.** Large corporations, consultancies and public bodies. Project-based, with many stakeholder meetings and occasional travel to client sites.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* enjoy both business processes and technology, and like structured, large-scale projects.
- *Think twice if you:* dislike corporate bureaucracy, long implementation timelines or heavy documentation.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in information systems, business IT or computer science.
- *Key skills:* ERP/CRM platforms, integration patterns, SQL, business analysis, project methods, stakeholder communication.
- *Credentials & experience:* SAP, Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics or MuleSoft certifications; ITIL; PRINCE2.
