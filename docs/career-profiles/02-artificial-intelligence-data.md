# 2. Artificial Intelligence & Data

## 2.1 Artificial Intelligence Development

**What the work involves.** AI developers build software systems that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, such as reasoning, prediction, conversation and perception. They integrate models into real products.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Design AI-powered features and choose suitable models
- Integrate large language models and APIs into applications
- Build data pipelines and evaluation tests for AI behaviour
- Monitor model quality, cost and safety in production
- Work with product teams to define realistic AI use cases

**Working environment.** Tech firms, start-ups, banks, consultancies and research labs. Fast-moving and experimental, usually office or remote.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* are excited by new technology, comfortable with uncertainty, and enjoy experimenting and measuring results.
- *Think twice if you:* want stable, well-defined tools or dislike rapid change in your field.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in computer science, AI, mathematics or engineering. A master's is common.
- *Key skills:* Python, ML frameworks (PyTorch, TensorFlow), LLM APIs and prompt/agent design, software engineering, evaluation methods, cloud.
- *Credentials & experience:* Project portfolio. Cloud AI certifications such as AWS ML Specialty, Azure AI Engineer or Google ML Engineer.

## 2.2 Machine Learning

**What the work involves.** Machine learning engineers build systems that learn patterns from data to make predictions or decisions. They take models from experiment to reliable, scalable production.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Prepare and engineer features from data
- Train, tune and validate ML models
- Deploy models and build ML pipelines (MLOps)
- Monitor drift, accuracy and fairness
- Work with data engineers and product teams

**Working environment.** Tech, finance, e-commerce, health and logistics. Computer-based, collaborative and experiment-driven.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* enjoy mathematics and coding together, and are patient with iterative experiments that often fail.
- *Think twice if you:* dislike statistics or want immediate, certain results.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in computer science, statistics, mathematics or engineering. A master's or PhD is often preferred.
- *Key skills:* Python, scikit-learn, PyTorch/TensorFlow, statistics, linear algebra, SQL, MLOps tools (MLflow, Kubeflow), cloud.
- *Credentials & experience:* Kaggle or project portfolio. Google Professional ML Engineer or AWS ML certifications.

## 2.3 Deep Learning

**What the work involves.** Deep learning specialists design and train multi-layer neural networks for complex tasks such as image recognition, speech, text generation and scientific modelling.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Design neural network architectures
- Train large models on GPUs or accelerators
- Optimise training efficiency and model size
- Run experiments and read research papers
- Fine-tune pre-trained models for specific tasks

**Working environment.** Research labs, AI companies, universities and advanced R&D teams. Compute-intensive and research-oriented.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* have strong mathematical curiosity, enjoy reading research, and can sustain long experiments.
- *Think twice if you:* prefer quick, practical deliverables or dislike advanced mathematics.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Master's or PhD in machine learning, computer science, physics or mathematics is common.
- *Key skills:* PyTorch/JAX, calculus, linear algebra, optimisation, GPU computing, distributed training, research methodology.
- *Credentials & experience:* Publications, open-source models or strong research projects.

## 2.4 Natural Language Processing

**What the work involves.** NLP specialists build systems that understand, generate and translate human language, including chatbots, search, summarisation, sentiment analysis and speech interfaces.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Build and fine-tune language models
- Create text-processing pipelines (tokenisation, entity recognition, classification)
- Design retrieval-augmented generation and search systems
- Evaluate language output for accuracy, bias and safety
- Adapt models to specific languages or domains

**Working environment.** AI companies, search and social media firms, customer service technology, legal tech and research labs.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* love both language and technology, and are curious about linguistics and meaning.
- *Think twice if you:* find ambiguity frustrating. Human language is messy and evaluation is subjective.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in computer science, computational linguistics or AI. A master's is often preferred.
- *Key skills:* Python, Hugging Face Transformers, LLMs, embeddings and vector databases, linguistics basics, evaluation metrics.
- *Credentials & experience:* NLP projects, multilingual work. Language skills are an asset.

## 2.5 Computer Vision

**What the work involves.** Computer vision engineers teach machines to interpret images and video, for uses such as medical imaging, autonomous vehicles, manufacturing inspection, retail and security.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Build image classification, detection and segmentation models
- Collect, label and augment visual datasets
- Optimise models for edge devices and real-time use
- Integrate cameras and sensors with software
- Validate accuracy in real-world conditions

**Working environment.** Automotive, robotics, healthcare, agriculture-tech and manufacturing companies. Can involve labs and field testing with hardware.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* are visually minded, enjoy mathematics, and like seeing algorithms work in the physical world.
- *Think twice if you:* dislike data labelling or hardware constraints.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in computer science, electrical engineering or AI. A master's is common.
- *Key skills:* Python, OpenCV, PyTorch, CNNs and vision transformers, image processing, C++ for real-time systems.
- *Credentials & experience:* Vision project portfolio. Robotics or embedded experience is a plus.

## 2.6 Data Science

**What the work involves.** Data scientists use statistics, programming and domain knowledge to extract insight from data and build predictive models that guide decisions.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Frame business questions as analytical problems
- Clean, explore and analyse datasets
- Build statistical and machine learning models
- Design and analyse A/B tests
- Explain findings to non-technical stakeholders

**Working environment.** Almost every sector: tech, finance, retail, health, government. Office or remote, collaborative with business teams.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* are curious, enjoy numbers and storytelling, and like answering "why" and "what next".
- *Think twice if you:* dislike messy data. Much of the job is cleaning data.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in statistics, mathematics, computer science, economics or a science. A master's is often preferred.
- *Key skills:* Python or R, SQL, statistics, ML, data visualisation, experiment design, communication.
- *Credentials & experience:* Portfolio of analyses. Certifications such as IBM Data Science or Microsoft Azure Data Scientist.

## 2.7 Data Analytics & Business Intelligence

**What the work involves.** Data analysts and BI specialists turn raw data into reports, dashboards and insights that help managers track performance and make decisions.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Gather requirements for reports and KPIs
- Query and combine data from multiple sources
- Build dashboards in tools like Power BI or Tableau
- Analyse trends and explain variances
- Keep data definitions consistent and accurate

**Working environment.** Business units in companies of every size and the public sector. Office or hybrid, with regular reporting cycles.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* are detail-oriented, enjoy spreadsheets and visuals, and like helping others understand numbers.
- *Think twice if you:* find recurring reporting routines dull or dislike stakeholder requests.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in business, economics, mathematics, IT or similar.
- *Key skills:* SQL, Excel, Power BI/Tableau/Looker, data modelling, basic statistics, business understanding, presentation.
- *Credentials & experience:* Microsoft PL-300 (Power BI), Tableau certifications, Google Data Analytics certificate.

## 2.8 Data Engineering

**What the work involves.** Data engineers build and maintain the pipelines and platforms that collect, store and deliver data reliably for analytics and AI.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Design and build ETL/ELT pipelines
- Manage data warehouses and lakehouses
- Ensure data quality, lineage and reliability
- Optimise storage and query performance
- Implement data security and access controls

**Working environment.** Tech companies, banks, retailers and consultancies. Screen-based engineering work, sometimes with on-call support for pipelines.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* like building robust infrastructure and solving scale problems, and enjoy behind-the-scenes work.
- *Think twice if you:* want visible analytical insights rather than plumbing.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in computer science, IT or engineering.
- *Key skills:* SQL, Python/Scala, Spark, Airflow, dbt, cloud data platforms (Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks), data modelling.
- *Credentials & experience:* Google Professional Data Engineer, AWS Data Engineer, Databricks certifications.

## 2.9 Data Architecture & Management

**What the work involves.** Data architects and data managers design how an organisation's data is structured, governed, stored and shared, so that it is trustworthy, compliant and usable.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Design enterprise data models and architectures
- Define data governance, ownership and standards
- Manage master data and metadata catalogues
- Ensure compliance with privacy and retention rules
- Plan data platform strategy and technology choices

**Working environment.** Large organisations, banks, governments and consultancies. Strategic and meeting-heavy, working across IT and business.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* think structurally, enjoy setting standards, and can influence without direct authority.
- *Think twice if you:* prefer hands-on coding or dislike policy and documentation.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in information systems or computer science. Usually several years in data roles.
- *Key skills:* Data modelling, governance frameworks (DAMA-DMBOK), database design, cloud architecture, privacy regulation, stakeholder management.
- *Credentials & experience:* CDMP (Certified Data Management Professional), TOGAF, cloud architect certifications.

## 2.10 Big Data Technologies

**What the work involves.** Big data specialists work with datasets too large or fast for traditional tools, using distributed systems to store, process and stream massive volumes of information.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Build and tune distributed processing jobs
- Manage streaming platforms and real-time pipelines
- Administer data clusters and storage systems
- Optimise cost and performance at scale
- Support analytics and ML teams with scalable data

**Working environment.** Telecoms, large tech platforms, banks, e-commerce and government. Highly technical engineering teams.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* enjoy performance tuning, distributed systems and solving scale challenges.
- *Think twice if you:* prefer small, simple problems or business-facing analysis.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in computer science or engineering.
- *Key skills:* Spark, Kafka, Hadoop ecosystem, Flink, NoSQL databases, Scala/Java/Python, cloud big-data services, Linux.
- *Credentials & experience:* Databricks, Confluent Kafka or cloud big-data certifications.

## 2.11 AI Research & Applied AI

**What the work involves.** AI researchers advance the frontier of artificial intelligence through new algorithms, models and methods. Applied researchers adapt cutting-edge research to real problems.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Form research hypotheses and design experiments
- Develop new models, training methods or evaluation techniques
- Publish papers and present at conferences
- Collaborate with engineers to turn research into products
- Study AI safety, robustness and alignment

**Working environment.** University labs, AI research companies and corporate R&D. Intellectually demanding, often flexible but deadline-driven around conferences.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* are deeply curious, self-directed, resilient to failed experiments, and enjoy writing and debate.
- *Think twice if you:* need quick, tangible results or dislike highly competitive academic environments.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* PhD in machine learning, computer science, mathematics, statistics or physics is usually expected.
- *Key skills:* Advanced mathematics, deep learning frameworks, experimental design, scientific writing, programming.
- *Credentials & experience:* Publications at top venues (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL, CVPR), research internships.

## 2.12 AI Product Development

**What the work involves.** AI product managers and product developers define what AI-powered products should do, for whom and why. They link user needs, business value and technical feasibility.

**Typical responsibilities**
- Identify user problems that AI can meaningfully solve
- Define product requirements, success metrics and roadmaps
- Coordinate data scientists, engineers and designers
- Manage AI risks: accuracy, bias, privacy and safety
- Launch, measure and iterate AI features

**Working environment.** Tech companies, start-ups and digital units of large firms. Meeting-heavy, fast-paced and cross-functional.

**Personal compatibility**
- *Good fit if you:* communicate well, like strategy and users, and understand technology without needing to code all day.
- *Think twice if you:* dislike ambiguity, negotiation or being accountable without direct authority.

**Professional requirements**
- *Education:* Degree in business, computer science, engineering or design. An MBA is sometimes valued.
- *Key skills:* Product management, AI/ML literacy, data analysis, UX understanding, prioritisation, stakeholder management, ethics awareness.
- *Credentials & experience:* Product management certificates (Pragmatic, AIPMM) and experience shipping digital products.
